## WHY THIS WORKS

### 1. Industry Structures Are Published
- TaskRabbit, Nextdoor, Andi, OfferUp, Roadie all have public Terms of Service
- These have gone through sophisticated legal review and litigation
- Researching and learning from them is how legal professionals work

### 2. Compliance Language is Standardized
- GDPR privacy policies all have similar structure (European platforms use nearly identical language)
- CCPA compliance has standard patterns (California-compliant privacy policies are consistent across companies)
- FLSA and FCRA have published best practices that don't vary much

### 3. I'm Drafting 90%, Not 100%
- I'm creating comprehensive, well-sourced frameworks
- External counsel gets 90% complete documents (not blank pages)
- Counsel focuses time on compliance audit, jurisdiction tweaks, and risk assessment
- Counsel still has full control and approval authority

### 4. External Counsel Still Reviews Everything
- No document goes live without external counsel approval
- Counsel can catch gaps, identify issues, require changes
- We're not avoiding counsel; we're allocating their time more efficiently
- Counsel provides compliance sign-off letter (protection for you)

### 5. Precedent Exists
- Large companies (Fortune 500) use this exact model:
  - Internal counsel researches and drafts
  - External counsel reviews for compliance
  - Cost savings come from focusing external counsel on high-value work
- This is not experimental; it's standard practice

## FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

**Q: Aren't you conflicted because you benefit from billing fewer hours?**  
A: I'm employed as a salaried senior counsel. My compensation doesn't change whether I bill 91 hours or 0 hours. This recommendation is based on what's economically best for OxSteed, not what maximizes my utilization.

**Q: Will external counsel accept reviewing "pre-drafted" documents?**  
A: Yes. In fact, it's preferred. Counsel would rather review 90% complete documents and make edits than start from scratch. They can charge less for review than for creation.

**Q: What if I discover during drafting that I don't know something?**  
A: That's the point of external counsel review. If my draft identifies an unknown area, we flag it for counsel, and they advise. External counsel is the safety net.

**Q: Can I hire external counsel to review my work, or do I need the same firm I'd have used for creation?**  
A: You can hire different firms. In fact, a fresh set of eyes (firm that didn't see your initial thinking) might catch more issues.

**Q: What if TaskRabbit's ToS has language that doesn't apply to OxSteed?**  
A: I'll customize. I'm using their ToS as a SOURCE, not a template. If their language about insurance doesn't fit our model, I'll adapt it. That's why external counsel then reviews for accuracy.

**Q: What if I get sued and someone says "Your ToS was drafted internally"?**  
A: Litigation risk is the same whether counsel drafted it or reviewed it. What matters is: (1) Is it compliant? (2) Did counsel approve it? Both are true here. In fact, having counsel sign-off is better than counsel just drafting.
