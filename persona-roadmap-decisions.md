### Group 2: Household delegate (items 51-100) — built 2026-11-19

- **D-34. Household delegate model: the household is the owner's account plus a delegates list; there is no separate household entity.** A delegate is a person the owner invites (by email, resolved to an account when one matches) who, after accepting, may act only within the scope the owner grants (`book`, `schedule`, `prepare`, `coordinate`, `view_records`, `manage_addresses`, `manage_tasks`, `view_calendar`). A delegate never pays, never changes the owner's account, never sees payment internals (booking reads for delegates carry no amounts, no deposit state, no payout data), and never acts outside scope (every household endpoint re-reads the live delegation row, so revocation closes access immediately). The model reuses the G-6/G-7 token/consent machinery in shape (invite token, accept/decline, expiry, revocation, activity log) but is a separate grant: a recipient is who the work is for on one booking; a delegate is who may act for the owner across bookings. A delegate who books on the owner's behalf creates the booking with `customer_id` = owner (the deposit payer) and `booked_by_user_id` = delegate, so the ownership label (item 54) always shows both. Flip: introduce a first-class household entity (shared billing, multi-owner) only by an explicit call; that is a different product.
- **D-35. Delegation lifetime: 90-day default, renewable, reminded.** A new delegation expires 90 days after the invite (not 7: a booking link is a one-off, a delegation is an ongoing role; 90 days is the recorded call). The owner and the delegate both see days-to-expiry on every delegation read; either side can trigger a renewal reminder, and the owner renews consent (item 83) for a fresh 90 days from the renewal date. Expiry is enforced live (expired rows grant nothing) and surfaced, never silent. Flip: change the 90-day TTL (one constant, `DELEGATION_TTL_MS`) or add auto-renewal.
- **D-36. Spending approval is a request the owner decides; a delegate never self-approves and no money moves.** A delegate with `book` scope files a spending approval request (amount, reason, booking); only the owner approves or denies it; a decided request cannot be re-decided. Approval is a record only: it never charges, never moves Credit, never changes a price. Flip: wire approved requests into a payment flow only by an explicit call.
- **D-37. Private notes stay private; shared data is explicit.** Saved locations carry `private_notes` (owner-only, never returned to a delegate) separate from `entry_instructions` (shared with the delegate and the provider). Entry instruction presets are a fixed list (`gate_code`, `parking`, `pets`, `stairs`, `key_location`, `none`) stored on the location, not free text the provider must parse. Flip: add presets, or let the owner share a private note per location.
- **D-38. Calendar-only sharing is a scope, not a separate product.** A delegation whose only scope is `view_calendar` reads the shared appointment calendar and nothing else: no bookings, no addresses, no documents, no tasks. The scope preview (item 81) lists exactly what each scope grants before the delegate accepts, and the delegate accepts the scope as shown. Flip: add a separate calendar-share link (token) instead of the scope.
- **D-39. Closure is a receipt, and a returning delegate is re-invited, never silently restored.** Revocation and expiry are immediate and total; closing a delegation writes a closure receipt (who closed it, when, why, what it covered, upcoming visits reassigned to whom) that both sides can read afterwards. A past delegate is brought back by a fresh invitation with a fresh token and fresh consent, never by flipping the old row back to accepted. Flip: allow reinstatement of a closed row (would erase the audit trail the receipt exists to preserve).

## Group 11 (551-600): equipment owner/renter

- **D-257. Rental money model (551-600).** Pattern: off-platform settlement with platform record-keeping. Rental payments are collected off-platform today (`toolBookingsController.js:242-245`); deposits are recorded, not moved. Consequences: 567/568/583/586 invoice and settle by record only; 588 deposit hold/return/withhold is a recorded status with a written reason, never a platform transfer; 599 reports gross only, no settlement leg; paid extensions (581 variant) are a separate money item, not built here.
- **D-258. Owner approval (568).** Pattern: Airbnb/Vrbo request-then-accept. Approval is a distinct accept/decline step that precedes invoicing: the owner accepts the request first, only then is the first invoice sent. Decline closes the request with a reason. The current code fuses approval with invoicing; the build separates them.
- **D-259. Identity check status (571).** Identity means the platform identity-verification state. A renter with no verified identity cannot complete pickup; the status is shown to both parties on the booking. Owners are identity-checked via the 551 application review. The builder reuses the platform's existing verification integration if one is wired; if none is reusable, the builder records the gap rather than introducing a new identity vendor.
- **D-260. Confirmation receipt vs checkout acknowledgment (570 vs 575).** Separate steps. 570 is the post-booking receipt (in-app view of what was booked, terms, invoice). 575 is a distinct acknowledgment at pickup (renter confirms receipt of the item as described), with the pickup condition report attached as evidence.
- **D-261. Permitted earnings (599).** Gross-only reporting in this wave; no settlement/payout leg while payments are off-platform (follows D-257).
- **D-262. Listing readiness vs activation status (552 vs 555).** Kept as separate items. 552 is the readiness checklist (photos, specs, pricing completeness); 555 is the activation-status surface (published/unpublished, why blocked). They may share a panel but remain distinct checklist vs status views.
- **D-263. Equipment owner application form (551).** Pattern: the consultant form (501) precedent — new fields reuse the existing answer types so the generic editor and admin review render them with no per-field UI. `equipmentOwned` is a required `string_list` (what they own, up to 20 items); `ownershipAttestation` is a required `enum` (`owned_outright` / `owned_with_loan` / `borrowed_with_permission`); `equipmentCondition` is a required `enum` (`excellent` / `good` / `fair`, the owner's attestation of condition at application time, not a per-rental inspection); `pickupLocation` is a required `service_area` (where renters collect). `serviceArea` stays required and first. Policy version `tool-owner-policy-2`; applications submitted under v1 cannot be approved until the applicant supplies the new fields (reviewer asks for the missing information; the existing `policy_changed` path). No grant-path change: approval still mints the grant only through the existing review flow.
- **D-264. Pickup eligibility check (554).** Pattern: fail-closed at booking, same as the existing availability/moderation checks. The renter chooses `fulfillment` (`pickup` | `delivery`, default `pickup`) in BookingModal. Server-side in `createBooking`: delivery is refused with `fulfillment_not_available` (409) when the listing is `pickup_only` or `delivery_available` is false; pickup is always allowed (no flag disables pickup). The check runs on the first read and again under the per-tool advisory lock, mirroring the existing `is_available`/`moderation_hold` re-check. The chosen fulfillment is frozen into the booking's agreement snapshot (`rental.fulfillment`); no new column or migration. The existing email-verified gate is re-checked at booking time: the tool lookup now requires the owner to be `email_verified` (the same gate browse/public already apply), so a listing hidden from browse cannot be booked by direct id. `onboarding_completed` is NOT re-checked at booking (browse-only gate, out of scope). No distance or identity rule: the listing carries no distance data and identity is item 571.
- **D-265. Listing readiness checklist (552).** Computed server-side from the listing row (`server/services/toolListingReadiness.js`, included in `GET /tool-rentals/me` as `readiness: { ready, checks, missing }`) and rendered in the owner dashboard as a checklist distinct from the activation status (D-262). Checks: photos (a non-empty `image_gallery` entry, or a first image via `image_url`); specs (non-blank name, category, description, brand, model, condition); pricing (daily rate > 0; when a deposit is required, deposit amount > 0 — the hourly rate is optional uplift, not required); fulfillment (pickup-only or delivery available, i.e. at least one way to get the tool). Ready only when all four pass. No stored state: readiness is recomputed from the row on every read, so edits are reflected immediately.
- **D-266. Browse date filter (562).** Pattern: Airbnb-style availability search. Both dates are required together (a single date is not a rental range, so it 400s instead of silently meaning open-ended); dates must be valid calendar dates (`parseDateOnlyUTC`, so 2026-02-30 is rejected) with end after start, matching the booking rule (`end_date > start_date` check on `tool_rentals_bookings`). Exclusion uses the same overlap rule as `createBooking` (`start_date <= end AND end_date >= start`) over live statuses only (`pending` / `invoice_sent` / `payment_confirmed`); a cancelled booking never blocks, and a booking whose last day is the requested first day still counts as overlap. Implemented as a `NOT EXISTS` join in the browse query, not a per-tool loop, so paging stays one query.
- **D-267. Capacity and size fields (563).** One generic field set for every tool kind, not per-category specs: `capacity_value` NUMERIC + `capacity_unit` (kg/lb load, L/gal volume, W/kW/hp power), `weight_kg`, and `size_length_cm` / `size_width_cm` / `size_height_cm`, all nullable and non-negative (DB checks + server validation). Capacity is a value+unit pair enforced at the DB and in `normalizeToolPayload`: a value without a unit (or vice versa) is rejected; a partial update inherits the stored half of the pair, so editing an unrelated field can never break it. Filters: `capacityMin` requires `capacityUnit` (10 kg is not 10 lb, so a unit-less minimum is a 400, not a guess); `capacityUnit` alone filters by unit; `weightMax` / `lengthMax` / `widthMax` / `heightMax` are upper bounds. Listings with no capacity/size data simply never match a capacity/size filter; the filter never invents values.
- **D-268. Pickup distance filter (564).** Distance is haversine in SQL (earth radius 3959 miles, acos argument clamped to [-1, 1] so float error on coincident points cannot error), not a client-side post-filter: the same lat/lng placeholders are bound once and reused in the SELECT (`distance_miles`) and the WHERE (`<= radius`). All three inputs arrive together (`lat` + `lng` + `radius`, 0 < radius <= 500 mi); a partial set is a 400. Listings without coordinates are excluded while the filter is active, never approximated from their city text. Ordering: `sort=distance` orders nearest first, and a distance filter with no explicit sort also orders nearest first; any explicit price sort still wins. The columns did not exist before this item despite the browse code probing for them: migration `20261124011100` adds `location_lat`/`location_lng` with range checks and a pair check, and create/update now accept and validate the pair (same merged-pair rule as capacity). The renter's point comes from browser geolocation ("Use my location") or manual lat/lng inputs; the old radius note claiming a city was required was wrong and is replaced. No geocoding vendor is introduced: city names are never converted to coordinates server-side.
- **D-269. Listing comparison (565).** Cap is 3 listings (server `TOOL_COMPARE_MAX` and client `TOOL_COMPARE_MAX` agree; a 4th pick is refused with a toast, not silently dropped). Selection lives on the browse page and persists in localStorage (`toolRentals.compare`), so a refresh keeps it. The comparison view is a table, not a new route: daily/hourly rate, deposit, condition, brand/model, category, location, distance (only when the browse carried it), capacity, weight, size, delivery, rating, availability. A value the listing does not carry renders "Not listed", never invented. The server leg is a `browse?ids=` fetch (1-3 numeric ids, rows in requested order, still under the public gates: unavailable, held and deleted listings come back missing, and the client drops them from the selection with a notice). No new table, no new money movement (D-257).
- **D-270. Availability calendar (566).** Pattern: Airbnb-style month picker. The calendar replaces the two native date inputs in BookingModal; booked days (from `GET /:toolId/availability`) and past days are disabled, and a range that would cross a booked day is refused in the picker with an inline notice. The calendar is a preflight only: the server still rejects overlapping ranges in `createBooking` (409 `conflict`), so a stale or bypassed client can never book a blocked date. No new endpoint, no new column.
- **D-271. Owner approval (568).** Approval is a distinct owner-only step on a pending request (`POST /bookings/:bookingId/approve` / `/decline`), recorded in `tool_rentals_bookings.owner_approval` (`pending` / `approved` / `declined`) with `owner_approval_at`. The first invoice is refused with 409 `owner_approval_required` until approval (checked after the idempotency replay, beside the existing terms gate, so approval alone does not bypass the terms gate either). Decline requires a non-empty reason (max 2000 chars), closes the request (`status='cancelled'`) and notifies the renter via the existing booking-declined email. The old owner-cancel path is unchanged. A renter deciding gets 403, a stranger 404.
- **D-272. Confirmation receipt (570).** `GET /bookings/:bookingId/receipt` returns the booking row, the tool, the terms-acceptance state for both parties and the invoice (or null before invoicing); party-only, strangers get 404. The client renders it as a lazy expandable panel on the booking card with a PDF download of the invoice (blob fetch, so the auth header still applies). No new email work: the confirmation email already sends at booking time.
- **D-273. Identity check status (571).** Identity is the platform Didit state, reused, no new vendor (`server/utils/rentalIdentity.js` over `users.didit_status` / `didit_verified_at` / `identity_verified` / `id_verified`). "Verified" is stricter than the general `isDiditIdentitySatisfied` gate: a `skipped` verification satisfies the general gate when enforcement is off but does NOT count as verified here, and a `verified` status older than the 2-year Didit TTL reads as `expired`. The status (`verified` / `pending` / `skipped` / `failed` / `expired` / `not_started`) is surfaced on both booking lists and the receipt for both parties, with a link to `/verify-identity` for the renter. The pickup block itself is enforced server-side on the 575 acknowledgment (403 `identity_not_verified`); booking is not gated, only pickup completion.
- **D-274. Pickup appointment (574).** The owner sets an optional pickup window on the listing (`pickup_window_start` / `pickup_window_end`, TIME, pair rule with end after start, merged with the stored row on partial updates like capacity); blank means no window. The renter picks a time (HH:MM) at booking for pickup fulfillment only, stored in `tool_rentals_bookings.pickup_time` and frozen into the agreement snapshot (`rental.pickupTime`). The server refuses a time outside the window (400 `validation_failed`), a bad format, and a time on a delivery booking. The slot shows on both booking cards, the receipt, and the owner dashboard handoff (date + real time, not the midnight a date-only start renders as). No per-day slot table: the window is one daily window per listing, and the owner cannot yet change the time after booking (deferred, not built).
- **D-275. Checkout acknowledgment (575).** `POST /bookings/:bookingId/checkout-acknowledge`, renter only: the renter confirms receipt of the item as described. Server-enforced preconditions: booking `payment_confirmed` (409 `state_conflict`), a pickup-phase condition report exists for the booking (409 `pickup_report_required`), and the renter's identity is verified per D-273 (403 `identity_not_verified`). The record stores `checkout_acknowledged_at` / `checkout_acknowledged_by` / `checkout_report_id` (the latest pickup report, either party's). Idempotent: a second call returns the recorded acknowledgment. Shown on both booking cards and the receipt; the report itself renders in the existing booking panel.
- **D-276. Return schedule (576).** Mirror of the 574 pickup appointment: the owner sets an optional daily return window on the listing (`return_window_start` / `return_window_end`, TIME, pair rule with end after start, merged with the stored row on partial updates); the renter picks a return time (HH:MM) at booking for any fulfillment type (the tool always comes back for a handoff), stored in `tool_rentals_bookings.return_time` and frozen into the agreement snapshot (`rental.returnTime`). The server refuses a time outside the window or a bad format. Return reschedule is renter-proposes / owner-decides: the renter proposes a new return date (after the pickup date) and optional time while the booking is `payment_confirmed`; a pending proposal blocks a second one (409 `return_reschedule_pending`); the owner approves (applies `end_date` + `return_time`, never `total_price` — no-charge only, D-257) or declines with a required reason. Approval re-checks the calendar and refuses with 409 `conflict` when the new return date overlaps another live booking for the tool. Shown on both booking cards, the receipt, and the owner dashboard handoff (date + real return time, plus a proposed-reschedule note).
- **D-277. Cleaning instructions (580).** Owner-written text on the listing (`cleaning_instructions`, 4000 chars, set at create/update), shown to the renter at booking time (BookingModal, beside the owner rules) and at return (booking card + receipt), and frozen into the agreement snapshot (`tool.cleaningInstructions`) so later owner edits do not rewrite history; null when the owner wrote none.
- **D-278. Cancellation recovery (583).** Record-only refund settlement on cancelled bookings, no platform money movement (D-257): either party proposes an agreement (amount, direction `owner_to_renter` / `renter_to_owner` / `none`, note) capped at the booking total, with amount 0 requiring direction `none`; a new proposal replaces a still-proposed one; the other party confirms it (the proposer gets 403) moving it to `agreed`; either party marks it `settled` once the money has moved off-platform. The panel also offers rebooking through the 584 alternatives or a fresh search; a rebook is a fresh booking with no linkage recorded to the cancelled one.
- **D-279. Replacement listing suggestions (584).** `GET /tool-rentals/:toolId/alternatives?startDate=&endDate=` (public): same category as the tool, free for the whole requested range (the 562 `NOT EXISTS` availability join over live statuses), excluding the tool itself, nearest first with the 564 haversine when `lat`+`lng`+`radius` are given (partial set is a 400), under the same public gates as browse; limit defaults to 5, max 10. Surfaced in BookingModal when the requested dates conflict and on the cancelled-booking recovery view (583).
- **D-280. Damage claim (586).** Either party files a dedicated claim on a booking (`amount` > 0, description, optional linked condition-report id); one open claim per booking. The other party resolves it: agree an amount (at most the claimed amount, 0 allowed) or decline with a required reason. Amounts are recorded only; the money settles off-platform (D-257), never a platform transfer. Claims open only once the rental is underway (`payment_confirmed` / `returned` / `completed`).
- **D-281. Evidence timeline (587).** Photo evidence attaches to a condition/damage report (either party's report on the booking) via the existing upload path (`uploadFile` / `getPublicUrl`, data-URL fallback when no storage is configured); the timeline is the existing report history (`readBooking`) now carrying its evidence rows per report.
- **D-282. Support escalation (589).** Rental-specific thread on a booking: category (`damage` / `deposit` / `condition` / `other`), opening message, status `open` / `in_review` / `resolved` visible to both parties; either party replies in the thread; an admin replies in the same thread and moves the status. Resolved threads take no more messages.
- **D-283. Resolution acknowledgment (590).** One explicit acknowledge/dispute action per resolution, by the party who did not resolve it: damage claim (filer acknowledges the resolver's decision; a dispute reopens the claim), missing-accessory resolution (renter acknowledges; a dispute clears the resolution so the owner can resolve again, version-bumped), deposit resolution (renter acknowledges; a dispute is recorded as a disagreement while the deposit status stands, because the money already moved off-platform, D-257).
- **D-284. Maintenance calendar (591).** Scheduled events per tool (title, date, notes, `scheduled` / `completed` / `cancelled`); completing an event can create a repair record (593) with the event's date as `occurred_on` when notes are supplied.
- **D-285. Safety documentation (594).** PDF/image upload per listing, owner-only, stored through the listing-gallery upload path; listed on the public listing for renters, on the owner's rows, and in an admin moderation endpoint (`GET /admin/tool-rentals/:toolId/safety-documents`).
- **D-286. Return-to-availability checklist (595).** Server-enforced on the `PUT /tool-rentals/:id` flip from unavailable to available (409 `availability_checklist_incomplete` with the failing checks): a return-phase condition report exists for the last rental (vacuous when the tool was never rented), no unresolved missing accessories, no open damage claim, and cleaning confirmed in the request. The checklist is readable at `GET /:toolId/availability-checklist`.
- **D-287. Utilization report (597).** Booked days are the days covered by live (non-cancelled) bookings overlapping the period; available days are the days in the period (the listing carries no availability history, so the period is the available window). Default last 30 days, max 365; per tool plus an overall ratio.
- **D-288. Repeat renter follow-up (600).** A repeat renter has 2+ completed bookings with the owner. The follow-up records a note server-side and the client opens the existing messages thread with that renter (`/messages?helperId=<renterId>`); no new messaging channel is built.
- **D-289. Permitted earnings summary (599).** Stays gross-only per D-261; no settlement leg is built in this slice while payments are off-platform (D-257). The tracker row states this explicitly instead of leaving an open gap.
- **D-290. Item photo gallery (559).** Max 8 photos per listing (`MAX_GALLERY_PHOTOS`, stored in the existing `image_gallery TEXT[]`, no new migration). The first photo is the cover (`image_url`, derived, never stored separately); removing the cover promotes the next photo; a single image uploaded on `PUT /:id` becomes the new cover (prepended, deduped, capped) instead of replacing the gallery, and `remove_image` still clears it. Gallery endpoints are owner-only, server-enforced by the tool's `user_id` (a stranger gets the same 404 as `PUT /:id`): `POST /:toolId/gallery` appends uploaded files through the existing single-image upload path (`uploadFile`/`getPublicUrl`, data-URL fallback under 2MB when no storage is configured) and refuses with `gallery_full` past 8; `PUT /:toolId/gallery` reorders/removes/clears and accepts only a subset/permutation of the stored gallery (no duplicates, every URL already stored, so a reorder cannot inject an arbitrary URL); `DELETE /:toolId/gallery/:index` removes one photo. `image_gallery` rides every listing read (browse, public, alternatives, `/me`). Client: the HelperDashboard tool modal edits the gallery (add/remove/move left-right/set-as-cover; changes save immediately on an existing listing, files stay pending on a new listing with the first as the cover and the rest uploaded right after create); the public listing renders the full gallery in `ToolDetailModal` (main photo, prev/next, counter, thumbnail strip); the browse card shows the cover plus a photo-count badge when there is more than one photo.

Territory launch plan (609).** Five default steps (confirm_boundary, complete_training, secure_location, recruit_team, confirm_opening_date), seeded at creation and backfilled, each with a due date (nullable `DATE`, validated `YYYY-MM-DD`) and an owner (`operator`/`hq`/`shared`); the plan rides the readiness status and is shown with it. HQ has full step CRUD (`POST`/`PATCH`/`DELETE /applications/:id/launch-plan[/:stepId]`); the operator updates step status only (`POST /my-applications/:id/launch-plan/:stepId/status`, owner-scoped) — titles, dates, and owners stay HQ-owned. `milestone_id` is a nullable UUID with no FK yet, reserved for the 611 location launch milestones: the plan surface works standalone and milestones plug in later. The launch-plan seed uses a separate existence SELECT because reusing the code parameter inside an `INSERT ... SELECT ... WHERE NOT EXISTS` makes Postgres deduce inconsistent types for it.
- **D-310. Operator-facing assignment history (610).** The operator history reads the existing `franchise_activity_log` (newest first, latest 50) through the applicant detail payload — no separate endpoint, no new table. The operator payload returns only `id`, `event_type`, `event_summary`, `created_at`: no `metadata` (an `application_updated` event carries the raw admin PATCH body) and no actor, so internal fields and admin identities never leak. `note_added` events with `metadata.visibility = 'internal'` are excluded; a shared note's arrival is still shown (its text is already visible to the operator via shared notes). HQ keeps the full bundle view, actor included.
- **D-311. Location launch milestones (611).** A location is a `franchise_locations` row inside a territory (D-298): one is seeded per application from the desired territory fields (`<city> location`, else `First location`), backfilled for existing applications by the migration; HQ adds further locations (`POST /applications/:id/locations`, name required, ISO-2 country when given), the operator never creates locations. A milestone (`franchise_location_milestones`: title, description, due date, owner operator/hq/shared, status pending/in_progress/completed/blocked) attaches to a location; HQ creates/edits/removes it, the operator updates status only (mirroring the 609 launch plan), and completing stamps `completed_by`/`completed_at`. A milestone may link to one launch plan step: the link is written onto the step's reserved `milestone_id` column (D-309) — a step carries at most one milestone (409 `launch_plan_step_milestone_linked`), and removing the milestone clears the link so the plan step keeps working standalone. Milestones ride both the admin bundle and the operator detail payload; a stranger gets 404 and a milestone from another application is not addressable through this one.
- **D-312. Opening team roster (612).** The opening team is a designation on the territory helper roster, not a second list: `franchise_opening_team_members` (application, helper, location nullable, role lead/member) marks helpers as opening-team members for one location launch, or for the whole territory when no location is given. The operator designates/removes from the roster (operator-only: an applicant record alone gets 403 `franchise_operator_only`, a stranger 404); HQ can designate/remove via the admin endpoints. The server validates the helper exists with an active account and a helper profile (404 `opening_team_helper_not_found`) and refuses a duplicate designation for the same scope (409 `opening_team_already_assigned`); the helper-profile check is in the controller, not an FK, because roster membership is best-effort city/region matching. `GET /territory-helpers` flags each helper (`opening_team`, role, location, member id) and the per-location and territory-wide teams ride the bundle/detail payloads; HQ sees the team read-only per location. Pre-existing defect fixed in this slice: the roster queries referenced `hp.service_area_city`, `hp.service_area_state`, and `hp.location_city`, none of which exists (`helper_profiles` carries `service_city`/`service_state` only), so every roster and helpers-KPI query threw and `safeQuery` swallowed it — the roster always returned empty. The queries now use the real columns.
- **D-313. Readiness review (613).** A formal readiness review is the only path that moves readiness to `ready`: HQ records it (`POST /applications/:id/readiness-review`, admin-only, outcome `passed` or `changes_requested`, notes required when requesting changes), the review is stored in `franchise_readiness_reviews` with reviewer + timestamp, and `passed` sets `launch_readiness_status` to `ready` while `changes_requested` sends it back to `planning`. A bare admin status set to `ready` is refused (409 `readiness_requires_review`) — except re-saving an application that is already `ready`, which is a no-op, not a move, so the HQ edit form still saves. A review on a launched, rejected, or archived application is a 409 (`readiness_review_closed`). The HQ edit form no longer offers `ready` (it shows only when the application is already ready, and cannot be newly picked); the review form sits in the HQ workspace and the operator sees the reviews (outcome, notes, date) in the detail page, never the reviewer. Activation is unchanged: it still refuses until readiness is `ready` and every checklist item is completed/waived — the review is the gate that opens it, proven end to end by the acceptance chain test.
- **D-314. Service availability checklist (614).** Each location carries a service availability checklist (`franchise_location_services`: location, application, catalogue `service_id` or free-text `service_name`, available, reason, updated_by). The catalogue is the linked business's active services (`business_services` where `business_id` = the application's `linked_business_id`), served as `business_services` on both payloads; a catalogue service is validated against that business (404 `location_service_not_offered`) and its catalogue name wins, while a free-text service needs a name. Upserts are idempotent per scope (one row per catalogue service, one per free-text name) via the two partial unique indexes, and both operator and HQ can save. A location launches (`POST .../locations/:locationId/launch`, operator or HQ) only when the application itself is already launched (409 `location_application_not_launched`), the location is still planned (409 `location_already_launched`), and at least one checklist service is available — a location cannot launch with zero available services (409 `location_no_available_services`). The launch is not complete until the operator confirms the opening (615, `launch_complete` stays false until then). Interaction found by the acceptance test: activation refuses an application with a business already linked, so a fixture business must be linked after activation, not before.
- **D-315. Opening confirmation (615).** The operator confirms a launched location opened (`POST /my-applications/:id/locations/:locationId/confirm-opening`, operator-only: applicant record alone 403 `franchise_operator_only`, stranger 404, even an admin 404s through this endpoint), with a valid `YYYY-MM-DD` `opened_date` (400 `invalid_opened_date` otherwise) and optional notes. The record (`franchise_opening_confirmations`, one per location, actor + timestamp) is upserted, so re-confirming corrects the date/notes instead of adding a second row (201 first, 200 on update; `opening_confirmed` / `opening_confirmation_updated` in the activity log). Only a launched location can be confirmed (409 `location_not_launched`). The launch is only complete once confirmed: `launch_complete` (derived, never stored) is launched AND confirmed, shown on the location on both operator pages and in HQ, where the confirmation renders read-only.
- **D-316. Territory invitations (616).** Territory-scoped, never business-wide: an invite names one territory/application and grants no business-wide access. One outstanding invite per email; 14-day expiry; token-based accept/decline with the invitee's account email matching the invited email, so a forwarded token cannot be used by someone else. The token is returned only on create, never in list payloads. An accepted invitee becomes a territory member, which gates 617 (manager assignment) and 618 (training assignment).
- **D-317. Training task distribution (618).** Training distribution is territory-scoped: the module catalogue is the existing restaurant training modules (`restaurant_training_modules`, picked by id with the title resolved from the module, or a free-text `module_title` when no catalogue module fits) — no second training system is built (D-292: the restaurant-brand franchise surfaces are not reused, but the generic training module catalogue is). Assignment and completion state live in `franchise_training_assignments` (application, location nullable, assignee, module, due date, status assigned/in_progress/completed), NOT in `restaurant_training_assignments`, which is restaurant-scoped. The operator assigns and removes; the assignee marks their own training complete (an accepted invitee is admitted even though they are not the operator), and the operator or HQ may also mark it complete. The assignee must be a territory member (owner, accepted invitee, or opening-team helper, 404 `training_assignee_not_member`); one active assignment per (assignee, module title, location), 409 `training_already_assigned`. Shown on the territory ops page, the operator dashboard, and in HQ.
- **D-318. Location manager assignments (617).** Manager assignment is HQ-only (the slice's absolute rule governs over the item text's "operator or HQ"): the operator sees the manager on the location card, HQ assigns and removes (`POST`/`DELETE /applications/:id/locations/:locationId/manager`, admin + `manage_operations`). One manager per location at a time (`UNIQUE(location_id)`); reassigning replaces the row, never a second manager. The assignee must be a territory member — the operator, an accepted invitee, or an opening-team helper (404 `location_manager_not_member`; the check is in the controller, not an FK, because roster membership is best-effort city/region matching, same reason as D-312). The manager rides the location on both the admin bundle and the operator detail payloads; the invitation payload carries `invitee_user_id` (joined on email) so HQ can pick accepted invitees as managers.
- **D-319. Coverage planning (619).** Coverage is a read-only rollup, not a new scheduling system: 14 days from today over the linked business's `business_staff_shifts` (cancelled shifts excluded), shifts and distinct staff per day, days with no shifts flagged as gaps. No linked business means an explicit empty rollup (`linked_business_id: null`, `days: []`), not an empty-looking one. Per-location coverage is the manager + opening-team count + launch state — not per-location shifts, because shifts are business-scoped and a franchise location has no separate business id. Operator- and HQ-only reads (`GET /my-applications/:id/coverage`, `GET /applications/:id/coverage`); the applicant record alone gets 403, a stranger 404. Shown on the territory ops page and read-only in HQ.
- **D-320. Territory announcement feed (620).** This feed is HQ broadcast announcements, not an activity feed of territory events — the survey's ambiguity is decided that way because the activity log (610) already records events admin-side. HQ posts (`POST /applications/:id/announcements`, title + body required, optional `expires_at`) and removes; the operator never posts (403 on the admin endpoints). The operator feed hides expired announcements but HQ sees all of them with an `expired` flag and a read count; the operator marks items read (idempotent, `franchise_announcement_reads`, one row per reader). Shown on the territory ops page and the operator dashboard, managed from the HQ page.
- **D-321. Standards library (621).** Franchise standards are franchise-scoped views over the Learn guides catalog (`franchise_standards`: application_id NULL = franchise-wide, else one territory; guide_id references `learn_guides(id)`, deleting a guide removes its standards). HQ curates (`POST /applications/:id/standards` per territory, `POST /franchise/standards` franchise-wide; the guide must be a published Learn guide, 404 `standard_guide_not_found`) and removes (`DELETE` per scope); one standard per guide per scope, enforced by a unique index on the coalesced scope + guide (409 `standard_already_curated`). No separate franchise library system is built in this wave (D-297). Standards ride the admin bundle and the operator detail payloads (franchise-wide rows plus the territory's own, guide title/category/minutes joined from Learn), browsed from the territory ops page and the operator dashboard, curated from the HQ page (guide picker over `GET /learn/guides`, scope select). Server-enforced HQ-only curation (a non-admin gets 403 on the admin endpoints).
- **D-322. Standard version (621/622/625).** A standard's version is an HQ-published integer starting at 1, independent of the Learn guide's content (Learn guides carry no version). Bumping the version is the policy-change event: it is what 622 acknowledges and 625 reminds about. Old versions stay readable in the acknowledgment history; reminders only ever target the current version.
- **D-323. Version acknowledgment (622).** Version acknowledgment reuses the SOP read-and-sign pattern: a territory member (operator, accepted invitee, or opening-team helper — checked against live rows, never a role label) acknowledges a standard's current version with a drawn e-signature PNG (required, ≤2MB, PNG magic bytes, stored via `uploadFile` under `franchise-standards/signatures/`; the storage key never leaves the server). One row per (standard, user, version) in `franchise_standard_acknowledgments`, so re-acknowledging the same version returns the existing row; acknowledging a stale version is 409 `standard_version_conflict`. HQ publishes a new version (`POST .../standards/:standardId/version`, per territory or franchise-wide); the reminder trigger on publish is wired by 625, not here. The acknowledgment list (user, version, date, has-signature) rides the HQ bundle; the operator detail carries only the viewer's own state plus acknowledged/member counts. Shown on the territory ops page and the operator dashboard (SignaturePad), managed from the HQ page.
- **D-324. Brand asset access (623).** Brand assets reuse the staff-document vault storage pattern: HQ uploads a file through the existing upload path (multer + `validateFileUpload`) into the private `franchise-brand-assets/<territory|franchise>/` prefix (`franchise_brand_assets`: application_id NULL = franchise-wide, else one territory; name defaults to the uploaded file name, description optional); downloads are signed URLs minted at read time (600s), never a public path, and the storage key never leaves the server in a payload. HQ uploads and removes (per territory or franchise-wide, admin + `manage_operations`); operators browse and download from the territory ops page and the operator dashboard. Removing an asset deletes the row and best-effort deletes the stored file — a storage failure never fails the removal. No new storage system is built.
- **D-325. Operator compliance checklist (624).** The compliance checklist is derived, never stored: one item per current standard, a team rollup item, and a launch-readiness item. An operator checks a standard off by acknowledging its current version (622); the per-standard item is viewer-perspective in the operator detail (did I acknowledge) and team-perspective in the HQ bundle (did every member acknowledge). The team rollup is always team-perspective — done only when every member acknowledged every current standard. The readiness item is done when the territory's launch readiness status is `ready` or `launched`. Shown on the territory ops page, the operator dashboard, and HQ, via `buildComplianceChecklist` in `franchiseController.js`. Distinct from the HQ intake checklist (603).
- **D-326. Policy change reminders (625).** When a standard's version is published, the members who have not yet acknowledged the new version are reminded immediately (the publish handler calls the sender with `force: true`, after COMMIT; a reminder failure is swallowed and logged, never a 500). A daily fenced sweeper (08:30 server time, `franchiseStandardReminders.js`, registered in `backgroundWorkers.js`) repeats at most once per person per version per day — tracked in the `franchise_standard_reminders` ledger (PK standard/user/version) — until the member acknowledges or the version is superseded; only the current version is ever reminded, and acknowledged members are never reminded. HQ can re-send now from the standards list ("Remind now"), an explicit HQ action that bypasses the 24h dedupe. Notification type `franchise_standard_updated` (group `work`) is registered in the notification type catalog with a localized template in `server/utils/i18n.js`; each send carries an explicit per-day idempotency key. Franchise-wide standards remind the members of every territory; territory standards remind only their own territory.
- **D-327. Service exception summary (628).** A franchise "exception" is a support ticket attached to a territory — no parallel exception store is built (D-293). Attachment is two nullable columns on `support_tickets` (`franchise_application_id`, `franchise_location_id`, NULL location = territory-wide); deleting an application or location unlinks its tickets instead of dropping support history. An exception is open while its ticket status is `open`, `assigned`, `in_progress`, or `waiting_user`; `resolved`/`closed` close it. Severity is the ticket's own priority: open `urgent`/`high` tickets are the urgent count. Attachment is explicit, never inferred: the operator links one of their own tickets by ticket number (location optional, must belong to the territory), HQ links any ticket; the generic ticket intake is untouched, so a guest can never plant a ticket on a territory. The summary is derived on read (totals, open/urgent counts, by status/category/priority, per-location breakdown, oldest open, five most recent open tickets) and rides the operator and HQ application bundles as `exception_summary`, with dedicated operator (`GET /my-applications/:id/exceptions`) and HQ (`GET /applications/:id/exceptions`) reads. The operator sees every ticket linked to their territory, including tickets filed by other territory members; a stranger gets 404, a non-operator applicant 403.
- **D-328. Per-location health view (627).** Health is derived per location on read, never stored: launch state + confirmation (614/615), milestone counts with blocked flagged (611), service availability (614), manager presence (617), opening-team count (612), and the location's linked open exceptions (628). Status rules: `at_risk` when any milestone is blocked or any open urgent/high exception is linked to the location; else `attention` when the location is not launched, has no manager, has any open exception, or has any unavailable service; else `healthy` (launched and confirmed, manager assigned, no open exceptions, no blocked milestones, all services available). Each location carries machine-readable reason codes (`blocked_milestone`, `open_urgent_exception`, `not_launched`, `no_manager`, `open_exception`, `unavailable_service`) plus a territory rollup of healthy/attention/at-risk counts. It rides the operator and HQ application bundles as `location_health`, with dedicated operator (`GET /my-applications/:id/location-health`) and HQ (`GET /applications/:id/location-health`) reads. The brand-side store comparison in `BrandHQPanel.jsx` is not reused: it is brand-facing and not territory-scoped. Per-location shift coverage is not part of health: shifts are business-scoped and a franchise location has no separate business id (D-319), so coverage stays a territory-level trend (630).
- **D-329. Territory ticket queue (629).** The territory queue is a filtered read over the tickets linked to the territory (628), not a second ticket system: `GET /my-applications/:id/support-tickets` (operator) and `GET /applications/:id/support-tickets` (HQ), both server-enforced to the one territory. `?status=` defaults to `open` (the four open statuses), accepts `all` or one concrete status, and rejects anything else with 400 `invalid_ticket_status`; `?location_id=` takes one of the territory's locations or `unassigned` for territory-wide tickets, and a location from another territory is 404 `location_not_found`. Every row carries its franchise link (`application_id`, `location_id`, `location_name`) and a link to the generic ticket detail (`/support/my-tickets/:id`), so the queue never shows a ticket without saying which application and location it belongs to. The operator sees every ticket linked to their territory, including tickets filed by other territory members; HQ sees the same per-territory queue read-only plus unlink. The generic `GET /support/my-tickets` is untouched — it stays the requester's own tickets, and the franchise queue is the territory-scoped surface.
- **D-330. Shift coverage trend (630).** The trend is a read-only rollup over the linked business's `business_staff_shifts`, never a new scheduling system: one row per day in the window (shifts, distinct staff, covered), 7-day buckets anchored at the window start (covered days, coverage %), and an overall coverage % for the window. Default window is the 28 days ending today; `?from=`/`?to=` (YYYY-MM-DD) override it, capped at 93 days like the schedule forecast, invalid ranges 400. Cancelled shifts never count. It reads the same shift set as the 619 rollup (non-cancelled shifts, drafts included), because the trend is the territory's own history, not a manager-only draft view. No linked business means an explicit empty trend (`linked_business_id: null`, `days: []`, `coverage_pct: null`), not an empty-looking one. Coverage stays territory-level: shifts are business-scoped and a franchise location has no separate business id, so per-location trends are not built (D-319). Operator (`GET /my-applications/:id/coverage-trend`) and HQ (`GET /applications/:id/coverage-trend`) reads, surfaced on the territory ops page and in HQ alongside the 619 rollup.
- **D-331. Issue escalation (631).** Escalation is recorded ON the ticket, not a parallel system: `POST /my-applications/:id/support-tickets/:ticketId/escalate` (operator) and the HQ twin bump the ticket priority to `urgent` (from low/normal/high; already-urgent stays), insert a `franchise_ticket_escalations` row (application, ticket, escalated_by, reason, priority_before/after), add an escalation note to the ticket thread (`support_messages`, visible to the requester and HQ), and write the territory activity log. A reason is required (400 `escalation_reason_required`, max 1000 chars). One live escalation per ticket: re-escalating while one is live is 409 `exception_already_escalated`; escalating a resolved/closed ticket is 409 `exception_closed` (reopen first, then escalate); a `reopened` event after the last escalation starts a new episode and may escalate again. The HQ queue is `GET /applications/:id/escalations` (operator twin `GET /my-applications/:id/escalations`), newest first, every row carrying ticket number/subject/category/priority/status, location id/name, and the escalator's name, so an escalation never appears without its territory and location context. The operator escalates only their own linked tickets (a stranger's ticket is 404 `ticket_not_found`, an unlinked ticket 404 `exception_not_found`); HQ escalates any linked ticket.
- **D-332. Remediation owner assignment (632).** One remediation per linked exception (`franchise_remediations`, unique on application + ticket): an owner plus a due date, both required. The owner must be a territory member — the operator, an accepted invitee, or an opening-team helper — checked against live rows with `isTerritoryMember`, never a role label; a non-member is 404 `remediation_owner_not_member`, an unknown/inactive user 404 `remediation_owner_not_found`. Set by HQ or the territory operator for any linked ticket in the territory (not just the operator's own tickets); reassigning replaces the row (upsert, 201 first / 200 on update). A bad due date is 400 `invalid_remediation_due_date`. The owner sees their assignments two ways: `GET /franchise/my-remediations` (their rows across all territories, due-date order) and `?mine=true` on the territory list (`GET /my-applications/:id/remediations`, HQ twin). The territory member list (user, name, email, kind) rides both application bundles as `territory_members` and feeds the assignment picker. Remediation reads are member-aware: the operator and any territory member may read, an applicant who is neither gets 403, a stranger 404.
- **D-333. Corrective action checklist (633).** A franchise corrective-action checklist per linked exception (`franchise_corrective_actions`): title required (max 500 chars), description optional, status open → in_progress → resolved → verified, server-enforced with the same transition table as the restaurant-audit workflow (`open: [in_progress]`, `in_progress: [resolved]`, `resolved: [verified, in_progress]`, `verified: []`; a jump is 409 `corrective_action_transition_invalid`, a same-status move returns the row). An action links to the ticket's remediation when one exists (explicit `remediation_id` must belong to that ticket, 404 otherwise), so the owner is always derivable. The remediation owner, the territory operator, or HQ may advance open → in_progress and in_progress → resolved; only HQ may verify or send a resolved action back (403 `corrective_action_review_hq_only`); a territory member who is not the remediation owner may not move someone else's action (403 `corrective_action_not_assignee`). Lists are member-aware (operator + any territory member read; applicant-only 403, stranger 404) with `?status=` and `?mine=true` filters, per ticket and territory-wide, and each row carries its remediation owner, due date, and evidence count.
- **D-334. Evidence submission (634).** Evidence attaches to a corrective action (`franchise_action_evidence`: action, ticket, application, storage key, file name/type/size, note, uploader) through the existing vault upload path: multer + `validateFileUpload`, `uploadFile` under the private `franchise-evidence/<application>/<action>/` prefix, downloads as signed URLs minted at read time (600s) with the storage key stripped from every payload (`presentFranchiseActionEvidence`, `download_unavailable` when no signing secret is configured). A note is optional (max 1000 chars); a missing file is 400 `evidence_file_required`. The remediation owner, the territory operator, or HQ may upload (a non-owner member gets 403 `evidence_upload_not_assignee`); HQ and the territory may read the evidence list (`GET .../corrective-actions/:actionId/evidence`, both sides). Evidence counts ride each corrective-action row (`evidence_count`), so the checklist shows whether an action is backed by proof without a second fetch.
- **D-335. Resolution review (635).** HQ reviews a resolved corrective action at `POST /applications/:id/corrective-actions/:actionId/review` (HQ-only): `decision: 'accept'` verifies it, `decision: 'reject'` sends it back to `in_progress` and requires a reason (400 `resolution_review_reason_required`, max 1000 chars); reviewing a non-resolved action is 409 `corrective_action_not_resolved`; an invalid decision is 400 `invalid_resolution_review_decision`. The review records `review_reason`, `reviewed_by`, and `reviewed_at` on the action and writes the activity log. The linked exception closes only when its actions are verified — derived, never stored, in `buildExceptionResolution`: an exception is `open` while its ticket is open, `awaiting_verification` while its ticket is resolved/closed but an action is still unverified, and `resolved` once the ticket is resolved/closed and every action is verified (or there are no actions). Reopening a ticket through the generic support reopen flips its status back to open, so the derived exception reopens with it; no franchise code touches the generic reopen path. The resolution read (`GET /my-applications/:id/resolution`, HQ twin) lists every linked ticket with its exception state, action counts, and remediation, and rides both application bundles as `exception_resolution`.
- **D-336. Candidate location tracker (636).** Candidate locations are locations inside a territory (D-298), stored in `franchise_candidate_locations` (proposed name, city/region/country, notes, status): the operator proposes (status `scouting`), HQ reviews. Status flows scouting → shortlisted → approved/rejected, with rejected → scouting as the reopen path; approved is terminal. The operator may only submit for review (scouting → shortlisted) and withdraw (shortlisted → scouting) — every other move is an HQ review decision (403 `candidate_review_hq_only`) — and may edit or remove a candidate only while it is scouting; HQ may edit until approval. An HQ status change stamps `reviewed_by`/`reviewed_at`. Approval does NOT create a `franchise_locations` row: promoting an approved candidate to an open location stays the HQ `POST /applications/:id/locations` step (D-311), so approval is a decision record, not a launch. The tracker rides both application bundles as `candidate_locations` (totals by status) with dedicated operator (`GET /my-applications/:id/candidate-locations`, `?status=` filter) and HQ reads; a stranger gets 404, a non-operator applicant 403. Shown on the territory ops page, the operator dashboard, and in HQ.
- **D-337. Territory capacity map (637).** No geographic map is built (D-294: the boundary is a structured area list). Capacity is a derived per-area view (`buildTerritoryCapacity`): for each boundary area, the HQ-set target location count (`franchise_capacity_targets`, one per area, upsert by HQ), the open and planned locations matched to the area, the candidates in each status, the staffing load (opening-team members plus managers on those locations), and the open-exception load. Matching is by city first, then region, then country, case-insensitive, against included areas only; a place that matches no area lands in the unassigned bucket, and the territory-wide opening team counts in the territory totals only. Utilization is (open locations + approved candidates) / target, null when no target is set — a missing target reports load without a utilization figure, never a zero target, and the target never gates launches or approvals. The view rides both application bundles as `territory_capacity` with dedicated operator (`GET /my-applications/:id/capacity`) and HQ reads; a stranger gets 404, a non-operator applicant 403. Shown on the territory ops page and in HQ.
- **D-338. Training resource planner (638).** No second training system is built (D-292/D-317): the planner is derived on read (`buildTrainingPlanner`) from the territory's training assignments (618), its member roster (operator, accepted invitees, opening-team helpers), and the active Learn program catalogue. Per member: their assignments with due dates, overdue flags, and next due date; per location: its team (opening team plus manager), the assignments for that location plus the territory-wide assignments of its own team, completion %, required module titles, and the team members with no training assigned; per module title: assignees, completed count, and coverage %; plus a territory totals block and a gaps list (members with no training, overdue assignments). Learn programs link by category: an assignment category matching an active Learn program category surfaces that program's slug on the assignment and the module row, so the planner points at where the training is taken. Overdue means a due date in the past on an assignment that is not completed. The planner rides both application bundles as `training_planner` with dedicated operator (`GET /my-applications/:id/training-planner`) and HQ reads; a stranger gets 404, a non-operator applicant 403. Shown on the territory ops page and in HQ.
- **D-339. Launch dependency overview (639).** No dependency graph is stored: the overview is derived on read (`buildLaunchDependencies`) from the state each slice already owns — agreement status, training assignments (618, location assignments plus the location team's territory-wide ones), staffing (manager 617 plus opening team 612), milestones (611), standards acknowledgments (622, team perspective; vacuous when no standards are curated), readiness (613, a passed review), services (614), and linked exceptions (628, same aggregation as health 627). Eight dependencies per location (agreement, training, staffing, milestones, compliance, readiness, services, exceptions), each blocked/pending/satisfied with a detail line. Only four block a launch: an unsigned agreement, a blocked milestone, no available service, and an open urgent/high exception; everything merely incomplete is pending, never blocked. A location is `ready` (ready_to_launch) only when every dependency is satisfied, `blocked` when any is blocked, else `pending`. The overview rides both application bundles as `launch_dependencies` with dedicated operator (`GET /my-applications/:id/launch-dependencies`) and HQ reads; a stranger gets 404, a non-operator applicant 403. Shown on the territory ops page and in HQ.
- **D-340. Expansion readiness notes (640).** The admin readiness fields (`risk_notes`, `hq_recommendation`, `market_assessment`) are not duplicated: they stay HQ-written on the application and are surfaced read-only to the operator as `hq_readiness_notes` on the operator detail. The missing half is operator-authored notes (`franchise_expansion_notes`: application, nullable location, author, body up to 2000 chars), created/edited/removed by the assigned operator only — territory-wide when no location is given, per location otherwise (a foreign location is 404 `location_not_found`). Notes belong to the territory, so the operator may edit or remove any of them; HQ never writes operator notes. Reads are member-aware (operator plus any territory member; applicant-only 403, stranger 404), the same scope as the 632-635 reads. Both sets ride the application bundles (`expansion_notes` plus, operator-side, `hq_readiness_notes`) and are shown in one view — the operator's notes next to HQ's — on the territory ops page, the operator dashboard, and in HQ.
- **D-341. Operator handoff checklist (641).** The handoff checklist is derived on read from live state, never stored: open exceptions (628 counts), open corrective actions (633 statuses open/in_progress, plus resolved-awaiting-verification), unacknowledged standards (622 team perspective — a standard counts when not every member acknowledged its current version), pending training (618 assignments not completed), and open locations (planned, not yet launched). The handoff itself is a `franchise_handoffs` row (`pending` → `approved`/`rejected`/`cancelled`): the outgoing operator initiates it with a named incoming operator and an optional reason; HQ approves or rejects (rejection needs a reason). Initiation grants nothing and moves nothing — the incoming operator has no territory access while the handoff is pending. Approval snapshots the derived checklist onto the row (`checklist_snapshot`) as the audit record. One pending handoff per territory (partial unique index); the territory must be `approved` or `launched` (409 `handoff_territory_not_active` otherwise); the incoming operator must be an active account, re-checked at decision time, and must differ from the outgoing operator. Shown on the territory ops page and in HQ, with the checklist riding both application bundles as `handoffs`.
- **D-342. Open issue transfer (642).** Transfer happens only inside the HQ approval transaction — never on initiation, never silently, never as a separate reassignment. Remediations (632) owned by the outgoing operator are reassigned to the incoming operator with due dates preserved; corrective actions (633) move with their remediation (their owner is derived from it, so no assignee column is added); escalations (631) move as a responsibility note. Support tickets are never reassigned — the ticket requester owns the ticket; only the territory-side responsibility moves. Every moved issue writes a `franchise_handoff_transfers` row (kind, ticket, remediation/action, from/to, due date) before the owner change, and the transfer log is readable by HQ, the operator, and both handoff parties (`GET .../handoffs/:handoffId/transfers`). Training assignments do not transfer: they belong to individual members, and the incoming operator gets their own.
- **D-343. Contact directory update (643).** The operator-maintained contact directory (`franchise_contact_directory`) is scoped to the territory and has two kinds of rows: roster-linked (a territory member, name/email read live from the user row, never stored) and manual (name + email required, for contacts with no account). The operator adds a roster member (must be a live territory member, one row per member per territory, 409 `contact_already_listed`) or a manual entry, edits role/notes/phone (name/email editable only on manual rows — roster rows derive them), and removes entries; HQ reads the same directory and never writes it. Reads are member-aware (operator plus any territory member; applicant-only 403, stranger 404). The directory rides both application bundles as `contact_directory` (entries plus the roster with a `listed` flag for the picker) and is shown on the territory ops page and in HQ.
- **D-344. Records transition (644).** Records transition by access/visibility, never by moving or duplicating files: the territory's records (documents, standards acknowledgments, training records, exception history, notes) are per-application, so the incoming operator sees them the moment they become owner, and the outgoing operator stops seeing them the moment they stop being owner. The approval transaction stores a records manifest on the handoff row (`records_snapshot`: counts of documents, acknowledgments, training assignments, linked exceptions, expansion notes, activity entries at transfer time) plus the checklist snapshot, and writes `handoff_approved` and `handoff_records_transferred` activity entries — the handoff record is the audit trail. Before approval the incoming operator sees only handoff rows naming them (`GET /franchise/my-handoffs` and the party-scoped handoff reads), never the territory's records; after approval the outgoing operator keeps read access to the handoff record that moved the territory, not to the territory itself.
- **D-345. Territory access removal (645).** Verified firsthand: revocation already exists server-side (`syncFranchiseGrant` revokes the per-territory grant and the legacy self grant when no launched application remains) but there is no explicit removal action, no UI, and no audit surface. The missing half is an explicit HQ removal (`POST /applications/:id/access-removals`, reason required): it removes the member's membership — an accepted invitation is cancelled, an opening-team row is deleted — revokes any per-territory grant the member holds (belt and braces; members normally hold none), and writes a `franchise_access_removals` audit row plus a `territory_access_removed` activity entry. The operator is never removed here (409 `access_removal_use_handoff`): operator access ends only through a completed handoff or an HQ owner change, both of which already call `syncFranchiseGrant`. No second revocation path is built; the endpoint reuses the same grant-revocation update shape. Removals are listed HQ-side (`GET /applications/:id/access-removals`) and managed from the HQ page.
- **D-346. Handoff eligibility (641).** The incoming operator need not already be a territory member — a handoff is how an outsider becomes the operator, and HQ vets them at approval — but they must be an active, non-deleted account, checked at initiation and again at decision time (409 `handoff_incoming_unavailable` if they stopped being one). The outgoing operator must still be the territory's owner at decision time (409 `handoff_operator_changed` otherwise); a handoff that names a stale outgoing operator is rejected, never auto-retargeted. Cancelling a pending handoff (outgoing operator or HQ) moves nothing back, because nothing moved while it was pending. If the territory's status leaves `approved`/`launched` before decision, approval is refused (409 `handoff_territory_not_active`).
- **D-347. Policy question intake (646).** A policy question IS a support ticket with category `policy_question`, created through the franchise endpoint (`POST /my-applications/:id/policy-questions`, operator-only), never through the generic `POST /support/request`: the generic intake allowlists its categories and has no territory context, so a policy question filed there would downgrade to `general` and never reach the HQ franchise queue. The ticket carries the territory link from creation, plus an optional location (must belong to the territory, 404 `location_not_found`) and an optional standard (must be franchise-wide or the territory's own, 404 `standard_not_found`, stored as `metadata.standard_id`); the question is the ticket's first message and the ask is written to the activity log. HQ answers in-thread on the ticket itself via the generic ticket reply — no second inbox, no HQ-side ask endpoint. Policy questions ride both bundles as `policy_questions` and appear in the 629 queue and the 649 case tracker as type `policy_question`.
- **D-348. Operations appointment (647).** Franchise operator appointments are dedicated HQ slots, NOT the consulting flow (D-300): HQ publishes slots per territory (`franchise_ops_appointment_slots`: proposed/booked/confirmed/completed/cancelled, with title, times, note, and book/confirm/complete/cancel timestamps), the operator books one, HQ confirms or cancels, HQ marks a confirmed appointment completed. One live booking (booked/confirmed) per operator per territory — the interview rule (604) applied to operations (409 `ops_appointment_already_booked`); a proposed slot that already started is unbookable (409 `ops_slot_not_bookable`). Only the booker cancels their own booking (403 `ops_appointment_not_yours`); HQ cancels any non-terminal slot. Confirm only from booked (409 `ops_slot_not_booked`), complete only from confirmed (409 `ops_slot_not_confirmed`); completed/cancelled are terminal. Every transition writes an activity entry, and the timestamps on the row let the support history (650) derive its appointment events without a second store. Slots ride both bundles as `ops_appointments` and are shown on the operator dashboard (book/cancel) and in HQ (publish/confirm/complete/cancel).
