### Group 2: Household delegate (items 51-100) — built 2026-11-19

- **D-34. Household delegate model: the household is the owner's account plus a delegates list; there is no separate household entity.** A delegate is a person the owner invites (by email, resolved to an account when one matches) who, after accepting, may act only within the scope the owner grants (`book`, `schedule`, `prepare`, `coordinate`, `view_records`, `manage_addresses`, `manage_tasks`, `view_calendar`). A delegate never pays, never changes the owner's account, never sees payment internals (booking reads for delegates carry no amounts, no deposit state, no payout data), and never acts outside scope (every household endpoint re-reads the live delegation row, so revocation closes access immediately). The model reuses the G-6/G-7 token/consent machinery in shape (invite token, accept/decline, expiry, revocation, activity log) but is a separate grant: a recipient is who the work is for on one booking; a delegate is who may act for the owner across bookings. A delegate who books on the owner's behalf creates the booking with `customer_id` = owner (the deposit payer) and `booked_by_user_id` = delegate, so the ownership label (item 54) always shows both. Flip: introduce a first-class household entity (shared billing, multi-owner) only by an explicit call; that is a different product.
- **D-35. Delegation lifetime: 90-day default, renewable, reminded.** A new delegation expires 90 days after the invite (not 7: a booking link is a one-off, a delegation is an ongoing role; 90 days is the recorded call). The owner and the delegate both see days-to-expiry on every delegation read; either side can trigger a renewal reminder, and the owner renews consent (item 83) for a fresh 90 days from the renewal date. Expiry is enforced live (expired rows grant nothing) and surfaced, never silent. Flip: change the 90-day TTL (one constant, `DELEGATION_TTL_MS`) or add auto-renewal.
- **D-36. Spending approval is a request the owner decides; a delegate never self-approves and no money moves.** A delegate with `book` scope files a spending approval request (amount, reason, booking); only the owner approves or denies it; a decided request cannot be re-decided. Approval is a record only: it never charges, never moves Credit, never changes a price. Flip: wire approved requests into a payment flow only by an explicit call.
- **D-37. Private notes stay private; shared data is explicit.** Saved locations carry `private_notes` (owner-only, never returned to a delegate) separate from `entry_instructions` (shared with the delegate and the provider). Entry instruction presets are a fixed list (`gate_code`, `parking`, `pets`, `stairs`, `key_location`, `none`) stored on the location, not free text the provider must parse. Flip: add presets, or let the owner share a private note per location.
- **D-38. Calendar-only sharing is a scope, not a separate product.** A delegation whose only scope is `view_calendar` reads the shared appointment calendar and nothing else: no bookings, no addresses, no documents, no tasks. The scope preview (item 81) lists exactly what each scope grants before the delegate accepts, and the delegate accepts the scope as shown. Flip: add a separate calendar-share link (token) instead of the scope.
- **D-39. Closure is a receipt, and a returning delegate is re-invited, never silently restored.** Revocation and expiry are immediate and total; closing a delegation writes a closure receipt (who closed it, when, why, what it covered, upcoming visits reassigned to whom) that both sides can read afterwards. A past delegate is brought back by a fresh invitation with a fresh token and fresh consent, never by flipping the old row back to accepted. Flip: allow reinstatement of a closed row (would erase the audit trail the receipt exists to preserve).
