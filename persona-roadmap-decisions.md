### Group 2: Household delegate (items 51-100) — built 2026-11-19

- **D-34. Household delegate model: the household is the owner's account plus a delegates list; there is no separate household entity.** A delegate is a person the owner invites (by email, resolved to an account when one matches) who, after accepting, may act only within the scope the owner grants (`book`, `schedule`, `prepare`, `coordinate`, `view_records`, `manage_addresses`, `manage_tasks`, `view_calendar`). A delegate never pays, never changes the owner's account, never sees payment internals (booking reads for delegates carry no amounts, no deposit state, no payout data), and never acts outside scope (every household endpoint re-reads the live delegation row, so revocation closes access immediately). The model reuses the G-6/G-7 token/consent machinery in shape (invite token, accept/decline, expiry, revocation, activity log) but is a separate grant: a recipient is who the work is for on one booking; a delegate is who may act for the owner across bookings. A delegate who books on the owner's behalf creates the booking with `customer_id` = owner (the deposit payer) and `booked_by_user_id` = delegate, so the ownership label (item 54) always shows both. Flip: introduce a first-class household entity (shared billing, multi-owner) only by an explicit call; that is a different product.
- **D-35. Delegation lifetime: 90-day default, renewable, reminded.** A new delegation expires 90 days after the invite (not 7: a booking link is a one-off, a delegation is an ongoing role; 90 days is the recorded call). The owner and the delegate both see days-to-expiry on every delegation read; either side can trigger a renewal reminder, and the owner renews consent (item 83) for a fresh 90 days from the renewal date. Expiry is enforced live (expired rows grant nothing) and surfaced, never silent. Flip: change the 90-day TTL (one constant, `DELEGATION_TTL_MS`) or add auto-renewal.
- **D-36. Spending approval is a request the owner decides; a delegate never self-approves and no money moves.** A delegate with `book` scope files a spending approval request (amount, reason, booking); only the owner approves or denies it; a decided request cannot be re-decided. Approval is a record only: it never charges, never moves Credit, never changes a price. Flip: wire approved requests into a payment flow only by an explicit call.
- **D-37. Private notes stay private; shared data is explicit.** Saved locations carry `private_notes` (owner-only, never returned to a delegate) separate from `entry_instructions` (shared with the delegate and the provider). Entry instruction presets are a fixed list (`gate_code`, `parking`, `pets`, `stairs`, `key_location`, `none`) stored on the location, not free text the provider must parse. Flip: add presets, or let the owner share a private note per location.
- **D-38. Calendar-only sharing is a scope, not a separate product.** A delegation whose only scope is `view_calendar` reads the shared appointment calendar and nothing else: no bookings, no addresses, no documents, no tasks. The scope preview (item 81) lists exactly what each scope grants before the delegate accepts, and the delegate accepts the scope as shown. Flip: add a separate calendar-share link (token) instead of the scope.
- **D-39. Closure is a receipt, and a returning delegate is re-invited, never silently restored.** Revocation and expiry are immediate and total; closing a delegation writes a closure receipt (who closed it, when, why, what it covered, upcoming visits reassigned to whom) that both sides can read afterwards. A past delegate is brought back by a fresh invitation with a fresh token and fresh consent, never by flipping the old row back to accepted. Flip: allow reinstatement of a closed row (would erase the audit trail the receipt exists to preserve).

Quote response deadline: 30-day bound, advisory, once-only notice (items 8/9/10).** The customer may set a response deadline at most 30 days out and in the future; past or farther deadlines are refused (400). The deadline does not close or auto-decline anything: when it passes, an hourly sweeper sends one `quote_deadline_passed` notification and stamps `deadline_notice_sent_at` so it never repeats. Quote comparison is across the customer's own requests (service lines + amounts + deadline + status, up to 3 at a time), since there is no quote-responses table. - **D-27. Map search is built on the existing MapLibre + AWS Location stack (item 2).** Correction of the earlier deferral note: the stack exists (`maplibre-gl`, reusable `AwsLocationMap` with markers/popups, already live on the food home page behind a lazy load). The discover search already computed distances from `service_lat`/`service_lng` but did not return coordinates, so the backend now returns the already-selected `lat`/`lng` on helper, business, and job results in both `/discover` and `/discover/popular`. The pins are the provider's service-area point (the same point distance was already computed from), not a home address; rows without coordinates stay list-only and are never guessed onto the map. The map loads lazily, only when the customer opens it, and does nothing when the AWS Location key is absent (`AwsLocationMap` returns early; the key itself is Emile's Coolify domain). Flip: hide coordinates for any result type, or gate the map behind a location permission.
- **D-28. Comparison caps at 3; businesses are not shortlistable (items 4/5).** Quote comparison and helper comparison both cap at 3, matching the existing quote-comparison cap. Saved providers use the existing `saveHelper` API, which requires `users.role = 'helper'`; business cards show Compare but no Save button. Flip: raise the cap, or build a business shortlist store.
- **D-29. Kit substitution suggestions are same-category, cheapest first, never auto-swapped (items 32/34).** The DIY kit picker builds from the existing kit templates; an omitted item whose product exists but is unavailable gets up to 3 available products from the same category, cheapest first. The order stays paid and no money moves; refunds stay in the existing money-approvals flow. Flip: wire approved returns into the refund flow.
- **D-31. Vehicle service reminders are advisory, derived from existing fields (item 27).** Oil change every 5,000 miles or 180 days (due if either signal is exceeded; due-soon within 500 miles / 30 days), general service every 365 days from `last_service_date` (due-soon within 30 days). Seasonal packs are idempotent by title dedup; accessibility is a first-class support category; ticket-insert type fix (items 29/40).** Applying a seasonal pack creates planned needs from the seeded templates due 30 days out and skips titles the customer already has open, so re-applying never duplicates work. `accessibility` is now an accepted category on both the ticket API and the conversation route, with a form option on the escalation page. While wiring the item-40 test, the ticket endpoint was found to 500 on every real-DB call (`42P08`: `CASE $6` leaves the priority parameter type ambiguous between text and varchar); the fix is a one-word cast (`CASE $6::varchar`), verified against the real schema. ### Group 3: Recipient and sponsor (items 101-150) — built 2026-11-19

Verified first: G-6/G-7 (commit 632b1ba25) already closed link delivery, acceptance, 7-day expiry, revocation, site-contact chat admission, and jobs carry-over. Items 101 (limited status) and 111 (acceptance) and 119 (booking-only history) were already real and are marked existing, not rebuilt. Item 103 was half real (the scheduled window showed; live arrival never stamped for a booking that never became a job), so this group adds a booking-level arrival check-in. Everything else in the group was absent and is built here.

- **D-40. Sponsor payment model (items 121-130).** A sponsor is a third party the booker invites to pay for one booking; the recipient never becomes the payer (D-10 stands). The sponsor pays with their own Stripe card through a per-sponsorship PaymentIntent, reusing the `mobile_booking` intent machinery: metadata carries `sponsorship_id` and the webhook settles the sponsorship row, never the booking's payment state. The booker's own intent is reduced by paid sponsorships, and a fully sponsored booking is marked paid with no booker intent at all. Sponsorship amounts are booker-declared at invite time, $0.50 to $50,000 like the booker flow; the total of live sponsorships may not exceed the booking's declared payment amount once one exists. Multiple sponsors may split a booking (128: each row is one allocation, and the breakdown lists every payer). Refunds return each payer's money to their own intent (129: `settleTerminalBooking` refunds the booker's intent and every paid sponsorship intent and charge intent, each idempotency-keyed). An additional charge (126) is a request the helper or booker files against a live sponsorship; the sponsor accepts (then pays through a per-charge intent) or declines (127), and a decided charge cannot be re-decided. Flip: settle sponsorships in Credit instead of Stripe (needs a Credit settlement path for booking deposits, which does not exist today), or cap a booking at one sponsor.
- **D-41. Payment responsibility breakdown visibility (items 129, 135).** The booker sees every amount; a sponsor sees only their own amount plus the fact that the remainder is the booker's; the recipient and site contact see who pays (booker, sponsor names) and never an amount. The breakdown endpoint enforces this per role server-side, it is not a client filter. Flip: show the recipient the total (would break the D-10 no-payment-internals rule for recipients).
- **D-42. Provider identity confirmation (item 104).** The token status page shows the helper's first name plus last initial once the booking is accepted, so the person at the door can confirm who arrived; it never shows a phone number, and before acceptance it shows no name at all. Account recipient and site-contact views show the helper's full name. Flip: show the full name on the token page, or hide identity until arrival.
- **D-43. Masked contact relay (item 117).** Every phone number in a non-booker booking view is masked to its last two digits. Parties contact each other through the relay: a short message sent through the server and delivered as a notification (and socket event) to the other party, never by exposing a number. The site contact already reaches the helper through the booking chat thread; the relay covers the recipient, who is not admitted to chat. Flip: expose full numbers to linked parties, or add a real proxy-number relay (needs a telephony provider).
- **D-44. Site actions are a record plus a notice (items 106-110, 141, 144).** Every recipient/site-contact action writes a `booking_site_actions` row and notifies the booker (`booking_site_action`); the booker never has to poll. Confirming a handoff returns a receipt (the action row: id, time, actor, details) that the booker and the recipient can both retrieve. A callback request (107) is also a `booking_callbacks` row with a target (booker, helper, support) and a preferred window. Flip: make any site action require booker approval first (today they are records, not approvals).
- **D-45. Contact replacement resets consent; removal after acceptance is guest-access removal (items 112, 147).** Replacing a recipient or site contact mints a fresh token with a fresh 7-day expiry, resets recipient consent to pending, notifies the removed party (`booking_contact_removed`) and the new party, and writes an audit row. Link revocation stays pre-acceptance only (existing G-6/G-7 rule); once the recipient has accepted, the booker removes guest access instead, which clears the link and notifies the removed party. Flip: allow revocation after acceptance (would strand an accepted recipient with no record of why).
- **D-46. Visit reminders are once per party, account parties only (item 102).** The 15-minute booking maintenance sweep sends one `booking_visit_reminder` per linked account party when the visit is tomorrow, recorded in `booking_visit_reminders` so retries never double-send. Named-only parties have no account to notify and see the date on the status page instead. Flip: remind earlier (48 hours) or repeat on the day.
- **D-47. Recipient and sponsor support is a support ticket, not a parallel case system (items 136-140).** The intake creates a real support ticket with booking metadata (`booking_id`, reporter role, kind), so triage, replies, and SLA ride the existing support pipeline. The scoped timeline (139) shows non-internal messages only. A missed-provider report (138) is a ticket plus a booker notice. A callback for support (140) is a ticket plus a `booking_callbacks` row with target `support`. Flip: build a separate recipient-case queue (would duplicate triage).
- **D-48. Care instructions live on the booking (item 143).** The helper or booker sets `care_instructions` on the booking; the recipient and site-contact accounts are notified (`booking_care_instructions`) and the instructions appear in their views and on the token page once the visit is done. If the booking converted to a job, the participant summary also shows that job's aftercare instructions (read, not copied: the job stays the source of truth). Flip: copy job aftercare onto the booking automatically at job completion.
- **D-49. The authorized receipt never carries amounts (item 150).** The recipient's receipt is available only after the booker enables sharing (`receipt_shared_with_recipient_at`); it shows service, date, status, completion and handoff evidence, and no money. The sponsor's own receipt (125) is separate and does carry the sponsor's own amount, because that is the sponsor's own payment. Flip: include the total on the recipient receipt (would break D-10/D-41).
- **D-50. Contact correction is name/phone only, auto-applied, recorded (item 149).** A linked party corrects their own name or phone; the booking row is updated at once, a correction row records old and new values, and the booker is notified. An email change is not a correction: it goes through contact replacement (112), because email is what resolves the account link. Flip: require booker approval before applying a correction.

Channel choice rides existing toggles for accounts, email for token parties (item 118).** Account recipients, site contacts, and sponsors choose channels with the existing per-type notification toggles (all Group 3 types are cataloged, so each has a toggle). A token party with no account registers an email on the status page for visit updates (reminder, arrival, completion), sent by email; removing the email ends them (146 covers the account opt-out; token email removal is the token equivalent). Flip: add SMS as a channel (needs a provider).

Booking requests gain a real `completed` status (item 105; sweeper fix).** Migration 602 locked the booking state graph from an observed subset and missed the delivered branch of `releaseStaleAcceptedBookings`, which moves an accepted booking to `completed` once its linked appointment is completed; the public status page also gates the helper door name and care instructions on `completed`, and the deposit service's `deliveredAppointment` helper assumes the same.
